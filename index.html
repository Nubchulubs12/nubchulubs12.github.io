⌘ How It Works

Understanding Your Mortgage Payment

Your monthly mortgage payment is made up of several components. Here's what you need to know.

🏛️

Principal

The portion of your payment that reduces your actual loan balance. In the early years, this makes up a smaller part of your payment but grows over time.

📈

Interest

The cost of borrowing money from your lender. Interest is calculated monthly based on your remaining loan balance and annual interest rate.

🏦

Property Taxes

Local governments assess property taxes annually. Most lenders collect them monthly through escrow and pay them on your behalf.

🛡️

Homeowners Insurance

Required by lenders, it protects your home against certain types of damage and loss. Many lenders collect this monthly as part of your payment.

📋

PMI

Private Mortgage Insurance is often required when your down payment is under 20%. It may be removed later once you build enough equity.

🏘️

HOA Fees

If you buy in a community with a homeowners association, you may owe monthly or annual fees for shared amenities and neighborhood maintenance.

Tips for Getting the Best Mortgage Rate

  • Improve your credit score before applying
  • Compare rates from at least 3–5 lenders
  • Consider buying points to lower your interest rate
  • Avoid taking on new debt during the mortgage process
  • Save for a 20% down payment to avoid PMI
  • Get pre-approved before shopping for homes
  • Choose a shorter loan term to save on total interest
  • Lock in your rate once you find a favorable one
ⓘ Frequently Asked Questions

Common Mortgage Questions

How is my monthly mortgage payment calculated?

Monthly mortgage payments are typically calculated using the loan amount, interest rate, and loan term. Many real-world payments also include taxes, insurance, and possibly PMI.

What is PMI and when do I need it?

PMI stands for Private Mortgage Insurance. It is commonly required when a borrower puts down less than 20% on a conventional mortgage.

What's the difference between a 15-year and 30-year mortgage?

A 15-year mortgage usually has higher monthly payments but lower total interest paid. A 30-year mortgage usually has lower monthly payments but costs more in interest over time.

How does a down payment affect my monthly payment?

A larger down payment reduces the amount you need to borrow, which can lower your monthly payment and total interest costs. It may also help you avoid PMI.

What is an amortization schedule?

An amortization schedule shows how each payment is split between principal and interest over the life of the loan. Early payments are usually more interest-heavy.

Are these calculations accurate?

This calculator provides estimates only. Your final payment may differ depending on your lender, taxes, insurance, fees, HOA dues, and other loan-specific details.
